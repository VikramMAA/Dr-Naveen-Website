## Who is usually considered

A joint replacement is usually considered when pain and stiffness limit daily life despite other treatment. That often means:

<Checklist
  items={[
    'Pain that limits walking, stairs or sleep',
    'Stiffness that makes everyday tasks hard',
    'Physiotherapy, exercise and other treatment have been tried and are no longer enough',
    'Examination and X-rays show significant wear in the joint',
  ]}
/>

Age alone is not the deciding factor. Two people of the same age, with similar X-rays, can need very different plans.

## How we decide on treatment

The plan depends on the person: how much the joint limits you, what you have already tried, your general health, and what you want to be able to do. Not every painful knee needs a replacement. Many people improve with non-surgical care, and some are not yet at the point where surgery makes sense. If I recommend a replacement, I will explain why, and what we expect it to change for you.

## Before you agree

Before you agree to any operation, you should be able to answer five questions: what exactly is wrong, what happens if you wait, what the risks are for someone like you, what recovery will look like, and what precautions you need afterwards. Read [5 questions to ask before agreeing to knee or shoulder surgery](/guides/questions-to-ask-before-knee-or-shoulder-surgery), and bring them to your consultation.

## Recovery at home

Recovery continues over weeks and months, not days. In general terms:

- You will usually be helped to stand and walk soon after the operation, often with a walking aid at first.
- Physiotherapy exercises are a large part of the result. Plan them before the date.
- Arrange help at home for the first weeks, especially for cooking, bathing and getting to appointments.
- Make simple changes at home: clear loose rugs and clutter, keep things you use every day within easy reach, and use a firm chair with arms.
- When you can drive, work or travel is discussed individually at follow-up.

If a family member abroad is planning leave to help, see [planning a parent's treatment from abroad](/planning-from-abroad).
