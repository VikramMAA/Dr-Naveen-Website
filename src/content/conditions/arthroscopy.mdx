## How it works

In keyhole surgery, the surgeon makes a few small cuts around the joint. A thin camera (an arthroscope) goes through one of them and shows the inside of the joint on a screen. Small instruments go through the other cuts to repair, trim or rebuild what needs attention. It is done under anaesthetic.

## What it is used for

Keyhole surgery is used for some knee and shoulder problems. The decision always depends on the examination, the scans and the person.

## What to expect

Keyhole surgery often means a shorter hospital stay than open surgery. The small cuts are closed with stitches or strips and covered with a dressing.

How long recovery takes depends on what was done inside the joint. Repairing a tendon or rebuilding a ligament needs much more time and protection than trimming a small tear. Physiotherapy-led rehabilitation is usually a large part of the result, so plan time for it.

## It is not always the answer

Keyhole surgery is not a treatment for every painful knee or shoulder. For knee pain caused mainly by wear and tear (osteoarthritis), it is usually not recommended. Many problems that might seem to need an operation get better with physiotherapy, exercise and time.

## When to see a surgeon

See an orthopaedic surgeon if a knee or shoulder locks, gives way or keeps coming out of place, if you have had a sports injury where you felt a pop, or if pain has not improved in a few weeks. Being seen does not mean you will need surgery.

## How we decide on treatment

The plan depends on the person: the exact diagnosis, how much the problem limits you, your work and sport, and what you have already tried. If I suggest keyhole surgery, I will explain what I expect to do inside the joint, why, and what recovery will involve, so you can plan for it.
