## Cast, splint or surgery?

Many fractures heal well with a cast, splint or brace. These hold the bone still while it knits back together.

Surgery may be advised when the broken ends have moved out of line, when the break goes into a joint, when the bone is in several pieces, or when a cast alone is unlikely to hold it in the right position. Surgery holds the bone with plates, screws, rods or wires, so it can heal in a good position and you can start moving sooner.

## When to see a surgeon

Any suspected broken bone should be checked the same day. See an orthopaedic surgeon for follow-up if you have been told you have a fracture, if pain or swelling is not settling as expected, or if a joint near an old injury still feels stiff or weak.

## How we decide on treatment

The plan depends on the person and on the break: which bone it is, the type of fracture, how far the pieces have moved, your age and bone health, and your work and daily activities. Not every fracture needs surgery. I will explain which option suits your fracture and why, and what to expect while it heals.

## After a fracture

Healing takes time. You will usually need follow-up checks to make sure the bone is healing in the right position, and physiotherapy to bring back movement and strength. In older adults, a fracture after a minor fall can be a sign of weaker bones (osteoporosis), which is worth discussing at follow-up.
